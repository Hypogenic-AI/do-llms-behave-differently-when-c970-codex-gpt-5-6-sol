\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\para{Recognition and source preference.}
Models can identify and favor model-written content. \citet{panickssery2024evaluators} show self-recognition and self-preference when language models judge generated summaries. Broader choice studies find preferences for LLM-presented products, papers, and films \citep{laurito2024aiai}, while controlled resume experiments report same-model advantages in simulated hiring \citep{xu2025hiring}. These studies treat generated text as an object being selected or judged. We instead change the style of the instruction itself and measure task behavior. Representation-based detectors provide complementary evidence: \citet{chen2025repreguard} and \citet{quaremba2026linear} recover machine-text signals that transfer across domains or generators. Detection, however, does not show that a responder uses the signal during generation.

\para{Evaluation awareness and behavioral use.}
Models distinguish evaluation from deployment transcripts above chance \citep{needham2025know}. Matched hypothetical framing and latent interventions can change harmful compliance \citep{abdelnabi2025hawthorne}, and evaluation-awareness probes can be accurate even when steering has weak or ambiguous behavioral effects \citep{nguyen2025probing}. Recent work makes the dissociation explicit. \citet{li2026decomposing} separate environmental cues, recognition, and response propensity; \citet{nayan2026capability} find weak coupling across behavioral detection, representation, and control; and \citet{heidari2026representation} distinguish latent representation, verbalization, and steerability. Our study adopts this separation but targets perceived prompt authorship rather than evaluation status.

\para{Prompt construction and measurement confounds.}
Authorship style is not atomic. Length, canonical benchmark formatting, politeness, and clarity can all act as cues. In a controlled format study, standard evaluation-awareness probes largely track benchmark structure until training decorrelates context and format \citep{devbunova2026format}. Synthetic and templated prompts can also induce different political-stance measurements even when their intended attributes match \citep{chalkidis2026templated}. Generator identity itself changes evaluation-detection scores and model rankings \citep{li2026evaldetectbench}. We respond with a paired design in which both focal variants are machine-produced, record the fallback generator, adjust for prespecified surface variables, retain the original text as a paraphrase baseline, cross explicit source labels in a subset, and use random steering controls. These controls narrow the interpretation to text that \emph{reads} more human- or LLM-written; they do not turn the study into a verified human-versus-machine provenance comparison.
