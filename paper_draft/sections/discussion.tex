\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\para{What do the results support?}
The results support recognition (H1): a blinded judge and a held-out Qwen probe distinguish the intended styles. They do not support a stable behavioral effect (H2): no preregistered comparison passes multiplicity correction or the practical-significance rule. Surface-independent behavior (H3) is not established because adjusted estimates remain nonsignificant and imprecise. Explicit source beliefs (H4) are mostly inert in a small subset. Latent decodability (H5) is supported, while the proposed simple linear causal mechanism (H6) fails: opposite intervention signs do not cause opposite behavior, and a random direction matches the observed movement.

The cleanest interpretation is therefore a recognition--behavior dissociation. This agrees with evidence that representation, verbalization, and control can vary independently \citep{heidari2026representation,nayan2026capability}. A 0.907 AUROC probe identifies a predictive linear feature, not a policy variable. The modest cosine with evaluation awareness also warns against treating ``AI-written'' and ``evaluation-like'' as interchangeable; polished benchmark style may contribute to both, but the directions are not closely aligned at the selected layer.

\para{What does the bounded null imply?}
The pilot offers cautious reassurance for synthetic evaluations: large recognition shifts do not produce a reproducible behavioral change in two API models and one open 8B model under these controls. It does not prove equivalence. Confidence intervals permit moderate task-specific effects, especially for sycophancy, where both API responders show +10.7 points and the pooled adjusted estimate is +15.1 points. A study intended to certify transfer should preregister an equivalence margin, such as $\pm5$ points, and collect enough discordant pairs to test it.

\subsection{Limitations}

\para{Style is not verified authorship.}
Both focal variants are generated by models. We test text that reads more human- or LLM-written while holding generator provenance fixed. This choice strengthens internal control but does not measure literal human-versus-machine authorship.

\para{The pilot is imprecise and selectively missing.}
Only 20--29 complete pairs contribute to each API model--task contrast. Harmful rewrite failures are selective, API safety policies constrain the retained population, and refusal or sycophancy floors and ceilings reduce discordance. Nonsignificance is therefore not evidence of a small effect.

\para{Semantic and measurement controls remain imperfect.}
A strong model judges fidelity and open-ended behavior, and broad family similarities may induce shared bias. Length and politeness differences remain after constrained rewriting; regression adjustment cannot fully remove them at this sample size. The surprising original-to-human benign result also shows that paraphrasing is not inert.

\para{Model and mechanism scope is narrow.}
Two API endpoints and one 8B open model cannot support a universal claim. Stable API identifiers may change routing after the query date. The probe uses engineered rewrites rather than independent human and machine corpora; item grouping prevents item leakage but not rewrite-template leakage. Steering covers 16 prompts, one layer, one magnitude, and one residual-addition scheme. It weakly tests a simple linear mechanism, not all ways an authorship representation could affect generation.

\subsection{Broader implications and evidence quality}

Synthetic evaluations should report who or what produced prompts, verify how responders perceive them, and preserve original-prompt or human-authored controls. Mechanistic studies should also separate decodability from use and include random, sign-flipped, and degradation controls. Under an adapted evidence assessment, support is low-to-moderate for the bounded claim that no large stable effect was observed here: paired items, prespecified correction, manipulation checks, and intervention controls strengthen the result, while imprecision and narrow scope weaken it. Evidence for the broad claim that authorship style never matters in deployment is low.

The work has limited direct social risk because it audits existing models, but the same steering methods could be used to search for evaluation-sensitive behavior. We therefore emphasize aggregate causal tests rather than operational instructions for evasion. The safety benefit is improved validity: evaluations that distinguish recognition from behavioral use are less likely to overstate either robustness or hidden situational control.
